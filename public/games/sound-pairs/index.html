Sound Pairs

A free English listening game for kids ages 7–12. Each round speaks one word, and the child picks it from two words that differ by a single sound — a "minimal pair" like ship / sheep or cat / cut. Seven levels cover the sounds learners mix up most: p / b, a / u, e / a, th / t, th / d, ee / i and v / w. A wrong tap plays both words back so the difference is easy to hear.

No ads, no sign-up. Part of childrendoenglish.com — free vocabulary games, flashcards and quizzes with Hebrew support.

Questions parents ask

What is a minimal pair? Two words that differ by just one sound, like ship and sheep or pen and pan. Hearing that one sound is the skill this game trains.

Which sounds does Sound Pairs practise? Seven levels: p / b (pin, bin), a / u (cat, cut), e / a (pen, pan), th / t (three, tree), th / d (they, day), ee / i (sheep, ship) and v / w (vest, west).

Why do children learning English mix these sounds up? Many languages do not have them. Hebrew has no "th" sound, and neither Hebrew nor Spanish separates a long "ee" from a short "i", so ship and sheep can sound like the same word.
